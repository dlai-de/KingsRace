    How to Play

    • Each player gets one King. Play against the computer, or name up to 4 friends and let the cards decide between you.
    • The four Kings race up the board, one column each. First one to the top row wins.
    • Cards come off the deck one at a time. The card's suit tells you which King moves: a Heart moves the King of Hearts up one row, and so on.
    • Every row has one face-down card. Once all four Kings are past that row, it flips over: an Ace pushes that suit's King up one more row, a Joker knocks both Kings of that colour back one row.
    • If the deck runs out first, whoever is furthest up wins.
    • Press Odds at any time to see each King's chance of winning from here.
    • Against the computer you also bet chips, poker style, on your own King.
    • Two players pay the blinds (a forced small and big bet) before each hand. The turn to pay moves round the table every hand, and the blinds double every 2 minutes of play — the clock stops when you pause. Run out of chips for the big blind and you're out.
    • You get 2 hole cards at the start and 1 more before each later betting round. They only show you what is not coming: a card in your hand can never move a King, so it is one advance that suit will never get. Yours are secret.
    • There are four rounds of betting: before the race starts, then after the 2nd, 4th and 6th face-down card flips.
    • On your turn you can check, bet, call, raise (3 raises per round) or fold. The blinds count as a bet already made, so folding is allowed right from the first round.
    • Use the Bet size slider to choose how much to bet or raise, from the minimum up to the round's limit or all your chips. A raise must be at least as big as the raise before it.
    • All in puts your last chips in and keeps you in the hand. You can only win as much from each rider as you put in yourself; the rest goes to the next King home.
    • Fold and your King keeps racing but can no longer win — the race runs on until a King still in the pot gets home, and that King's owner takes the pot.
